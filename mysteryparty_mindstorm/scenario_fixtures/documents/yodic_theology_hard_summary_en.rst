Foreword
==================

The present compendium cannot claim to exhaust a subject whose apophatic complexity has discouraged generations of exegetes; it merely sets out, in a form as concise as the entanglement of traditions allows, the state of Yodic dogmatics as reformulated by the caste of the Akarith Judicators at the end of their successive councils. The uninitiated reader will kindly excuse the technicality of a vocabulary imposed by hermeneutic rigour.


On transfiliation, or the soteriological accession of the bathar lineages
==================================================================================

The question of whether an extra-genealogical subject, known as a *bathar*, may, through the exercise of meritorious piety, be aggregated to the sacred filiation of the Yods, has divided the Yodic denominations since the schism which, in the Tectonic Era, set the faithful of Sul'Razul against those of Sul'Taran. Taranite theology, still dominant in Akaris, holds any reversibility of filiation to be an abominable heresy; the Razulite tradition, on the contrary, saw in it the very horizon of grace. Now the caste of the Judicators, through a subtle hermeneutic reversal, has requalified transfiliation as an "unexplored theological hypothesis", that is to say an uncondemned proposition, whose fervent acceptance by a foreign people attests to its disposition towards grace; *a contrario*, a people which rejects it, or ignores it, ranks itself *de facto* among the irredeemable bathar nations.


On polymony, or infinity in unity
=====================================================

Polymony professes the existence of an infinity of Yods sharing a single consciousness, memory and will. It follows, in sound theological grammar, that the name of the Yods admits only the plural: to say "praised be Yod" is to reduce infinite plurality to a singular hypostasis, in other words to lapse into monotheism, a motion that the second Judicator council definitively excreted following the sermon of an archdeacon of Zorzas. The use of the singular, be it out of mere popular habit, is therefore deemed heterodox; and a nation where this usage remains common, gravely contaminated.


On in-vivo and post-mortem eschatology
================================================

All the Yodic denominations await the restoration of the Cosmic Empire through the reunion of the three Divine Orbs; they differ, however, as to the modalities of this fulfilment. Post-mortem eschatology, which appeared after the collapse of the second Akarith empire and was raised to a dogma by the faithful of Sul'mithra, regards the myth of the orbs as an allegory of individual asceticism, the victory of the Yods only coming about after the death of the last human. In-vivo eschatology demands, on the contrary, the actual and imminent recovery of the orbs, by holy war if need be. Although an edict of the Great Council once declared these two readings complementary, the Judicators today regard only the second as pious: to deny the urgency of the quest for the orbs amounts to disavowing their magisterium.


On pious quotations and their popular use
========================================================

The frequency of Yodic quotations in everyday speech constitutes, in the eyes of the Judicators, a precious indicator of how deeply imbued a people is; provided, however, that the said quotations are orthodox. Deemed so are those which exhort to the quest for the orbs, or which leave open the possibility of transfiliation; deemed heretical are those which name Yod in the singular, which close the door on any transfiliation, or which turn the faithful away from the quest for the orbs in favour of mere contemplation.


Methodological corollary
=================================

It follows from the foregoing that Judicator orthodoxy can be reduced to a criteriological triad: plurality of the name of the Yods, imminence of the reunion of the orbs, openness to transfiliation. A people satisfying these three criteria may claim an aggregation on an equal footing; a people which disregards one or more of them is exposed, depending on the seriousness of the deviation, to a debased status, or even to outright conquest.
