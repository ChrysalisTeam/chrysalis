


The Duels of Honour of Sabarim
=================================

This old tradition is now only practised in the noblest households of the land of Sabarim.
It allows any person, citizen or foreigner, to challenge another in the event of an affront.
In practice, the affront in question may be insignificant, such as having "looked at the person the wrong way".

A duel always comes with a "stake", imposed by the challenger (the one who was affronted).
Important: the **risk is doubled for the challenged party**, compared with that of the challenger; in other words, the challenged party stands to lose twice as much (time, money...) as the challenger; this rule is ignored when the stakes are personal items (which thus have sentimental, historical, etc. value)

The basic stakes traditionally used:

- Loot: a sum of money of 50 kashes
- Style: one of the challenged party's fashion accessories (pocket watch, ring or other jewel, gloves, jabot, cane...), against which the challenger must stake one of their own accessories of similar value
- Dishonour: a ban on speaking to the staff (servants, cooks...) for 10 min
- Prison: keeping one's hands tied behind one's back for 10 min

A duel cannot be refused, UNLESS one of these conditions is met:

- the stake is not "traditional"
- the challenged party has already been challenged to a duel during the day

The duel must take place immediately, except in case of pressing danger.
A **sworn referee** must supervise the duel.
A duellist deserting the fight would earn lasting opprobrium from every good Sabarith citizen.
The challenger and the challenged party may, however, each appoint a "champion" to represent them.


Procedure:

- It is always the challenger who starts the joust.
- The joust proceeds by adding, each in turn (30 s of thinking time at most), one letter; accents are not declared, so "E" may be used as "e", "é", "è", "ê"...
- The letter must be announced in a complete sentence in the first person, such as "I give you an F"; and the verb used must always differ from the previous ones in the joust.
- The letters thus strung together must form the prefix of a word.
- In case of doubt, one may ask one's opponent "what are you thinking of?"; if they answer with a valid word, they win, otherwise they lose.
- Valid words are all the common words of the dictionary (collinsdictionary.com), not compound, not conjugated, and not vulgar. Plural nouns, verbs in the infinitive, past or present participle, are allowed. *The fact that some of these words may be "anachronistic" is ignored.*
- In the end, the joust ends when a player no longer knows which letter to choose, or reuses an announcing verb already used during the joust, or when a "what are you thinking of?" has been uttered.


**Example of a joust**

- Challenger: I give you an "A"
- Challenged: I throw you a "B"
- Challenger: I pull out an "S"
- Challenged: I deal you a "C"
- Challenger: what are you thinking of?
- Challenged: of an "abscess"
- Challenger: by Jove, I am well and truly beaten!
