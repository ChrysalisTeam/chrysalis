Learning and keeping track of the scenario
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

- **Read your character sheets carefully**, along with any other useful document; feel free to highlight the passages that seem important to you, and to print them out - these papers will be treated as out-of-roleplay on site. You may take a short **break during the game** to re-read them, and check whether some information or abilities have now become useful.
- The **items or web portals** specific to your roles will be provided to you on the day
- Bring something to take notes with during the game (pen and notepad), if you wish.
- For simplicity's sake, character sheets are written in the masculine by default, but most roles are ungendered; talk to the organisers if you have preferences in this respect.


In-game and out-of-game items (IC/OOC)
+++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

- Many items are present in the game; they are there to be inspected and handled, but **gently**, as some are fragile (glassware in particular).
- If an item bears a label with a "do not touch" symbol (crossed-out hand), it is too breakable and purely decorative.
- **The food and drinks on the buffet are out-of-roleplay**, so you can safely enjoy them to your heart's content.
- Some papers (Game Master's documents, character sheets) are automatically out-of-roleplay and confidential.
- When furniture or items are covered with an entirely black cloth, they are explicitly OOC and therefore contain nothing of interest for the game. Likewise, when items are in a plain black bag, they have not (yet) materialised in the game world, and must not be touched.
- **Black tape** may also be used to mark items as welded together, sealed, or fixed to the floor; in that case, do not move them, unless specific abilities allow you to alter these items.
- Thick hemp rope or black sheets may be used to materialise blocked passages, whether because of an impassable wall or because of a spell that must first be dispelled.
- Markers such as sticker stars or coloured feathers may be used to annotate game items. Do not remove them. If you do not know their meaning, you should not describe them to another player (as these markers are out-of-game), but you may ask them to *"take a look, because this item seems strange/trapped/magical to you"*. The methods for deciphering these markers cannot be passed on between players, as they represent abilities that take a long time to acquire in the game universe. On the other hand, ignore all the round coloured sticker dots, as they are only used for tidying items away.
- Modern books (e.g. the library's usual ones) are to be regarded as grimoires written in languages you cannot understand.
- Contemporary items (LED lamps...) are treated as their equivalents in a retro-futuristic "steampunk" world, or ignored if they are high-tech (computers...). Modern sound systems and mood lighting are out-of-game and are to be ignored.
- If in doubt about what is in-game or not, ask the GM or the NPC actors.

Abilities, items and traps
+++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

- Your character's special abilities are not your only assets. Many of the actions needed to progress in the game are open to everyone: searching for items and clues, talking with NPC actors, solving puzzles...

- The Game Master must be kept informed of special abilities that are interactive between characters (such as oaths), or of manoeuvres that alter the state of game items (minesweeping, explosives...).

- Feel free to take the Game Master aside during the game, if a rule is unclear, or to ask whether this or that is possible in this universe.

- Caskets and suitcases placed directly on the floor or on a table are considered **immovable**; they must not be moved, let alone shaken to gauge their contents (again, there is a risk of fragile items or mechanisms); small caskets and boxes placed inside other storage units may, on the other hand, be moved (with care).

- To ensure that information is well distributed among players, and to avoid losing items, you are strongly advised **not to take items you have no immediate use for**; for instance the books, artefacts, potions and keys laid out in the various locations. That way, the other players can study them too, and take part in solving the puzzles (this is first and foremost a cooperative game). On the other hand, when you find an item useful to your special abilities or to the progress of the quests, you may take it with you, but take care to inform the others of what you possess, and of the new capabilities it gives you. Riches (coins, diamonds...) may be taken freely.

- Some caskets or drawers in the game are fitted with a locking system. Do not try to force them, as they require either special abilities to be opened (e.g. magnetic systems), or finding, as the case may be:

  - Their metal key (if it is a keyed lock or padlock)
  - Their secret code (if it has numbered dials)

- Unless otherwise stated, potion vials represent a **single dose**, and are empty as soon as they have been drunk or poured for any purpose whatsoever.

- Once you have used up an item for good - for instance a padlock key or a potion - you may put it in the **silver bucket** on the buffet table, to avoid misplacing it.

- (Fake) candles and other "in-game" light sources may be moved, unless they are marked as anchored to the floor with black tape.

- Beware of lighting (LED garlands, diodes, candles) that may be lying on the floor; do not step on it.

- **Traps** are always visually detectable, or else signalled by various clues such as the out-of-game markers. You will not be penalised for opening some random book, or uttering a harmless word. On the other hand, if you step onto a pentacle or recite incantations you have found without understanding them, beware.


Fictional, "in-game" injuries and deaths
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

Your character may get **injured** during the game. You must then stop, lie down on the floor if possible, and call for help. The Game Master will then tell you the penalty you suffer.

If you set off a **light flash**, a **cicada sound**, or make **jingle bells** ring, it probably means a trap. Here too, call on the Game Master to find out what to do. {% fact "cicada_or_minibell_sound_mean_injury" %}

More rarely, your character may also get killed. But don't panic, this does not mean the end of the game for you, because in this game's universe, the world of the dead also offers many possibilities.


Theatrical scenes
+++++++++++++++++++++++

When music rings out, it is best to go and see what is happening, as it is probably a somewhat theatrical event.

You may be asked to dim or switch off the lights you are carrying, to enhance the atmosphere and avoid glare, for instance during a magic ritual.

During such a scene, especially if it is accompanied by background music, avoid making noise, interrupting it, or otherwise disturbing it. It may happen, however, that the protagonists of the scene call for your participation.
