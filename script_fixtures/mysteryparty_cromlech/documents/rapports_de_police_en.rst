Initial Investigation Report
################################

No sign of forced entry on the doors or windows of the building.

All doors open with the same key.

No other means of access possible.

Pool of dried blood on the floor in the Antiquities Room.

Approximately 1 litre spilled.

Blood loss dangerous but not necessarily fatal.

An ancient gladius belonging to the museum has disappeared.

No other museum property has been reported missing.

Investigation Update #1
################################

The key to the Cromlech is in principle held only by the Curator and the Museum Guide.
But its serial number appeared in a duplication request made at the cobbler's in the city centre, 32 days ago. A 3rd key must therefore now be in circulation.

One of our informants has let slip a lead: a very old gladius was left, for the space of one night, with a forger in the suburbs, so that he could carry out a quick analysis and dating of it. This took place 27 days ago. The requester had a build similar to that of {% symbol "Ryan Parker" for "nom_du_reporter" %}. We are applying pressure to obtain a copy of the results of this nocturnal study.

The Curator was indeed seen this morning at around 10:30 by journalists from Le Gramophone Lettré, in conversation with the Burgomaster, during the cocktail reception following the Maurice Genevoix literary prize-giving ceremony. The ceremony itself, which began at 9:00, was quickly marked by a scene: the author Eloi de Frontignan, furious at having lost the novel competition, tried to snatch the bouquet of flowers from the hands of the winner, Lilie Dupain. But the rest of the festivities went off without a hitch.

We found a piece of paper hidden in the lining of {% symbol "Camille du Guesclin" for "nom_du_conservateur" %}'s wallet. Scrawled on it is: *"clue: birth year reversed"*.

The blood spilled in the artefacts room has been identified as blood type A+.
It is less than 24 hours old.

Investigation Update #2
################################

We have obtained the report of the analysis of the gladius, carried out a month ago by a forger.
It certifies that this piece was genuinely forged using techniques dating back about 2 millennia, and specific to the Roman legions.

Very old liturgical paraphernalia was found hidden in {% symbol "Camille du Guesclin" for "nom_du_conservateur" %}'s office: a linen toga with a few traces of blood (type: AB-), a clay cup for ritual ablutions (still damp), a censer, a holly branch. Engraved on the censer are the words: *"the cryptex code is {% symbol "secret" for "code_du_cryptex" %}"*.

A misplaced, unstamped envelope had been put in the museum's Lost and Found by {% symbol "Eden Schmitt" for "nom_du_guide" %} this morning. It is a "farewell letter", written with the left hand, in which Andrea announces his wish to leave this cruel world. We are currently having this missive examined by experts, and deciphering the coded message scribbled on its envelope.

Investigation Update #3
################################

A revolver was found in the Treasure Room, behind a pile of books. One bullet is missing from the cylinder.
The fingerprints of {% symbol "Andrea di Revel" for "nom_du_héros" %} were found on the grip, on top of other prints which, for their part, appear to belong to {% symbol "Camille du Guesclin" for "nom_du_conservateur" %}.

The fake grimoire in the Treasure Room has recently been giving off vibrations that everyone can feel.

We have questioned various regulars of the Cromlech; they say they have noticed nothing abnormal there; but several complain of restless sleep since they started coming regularly, with dreams laden with ancient symbols.

The coded message, scribbled on the envelope found on the floor, has been deciphered: "Ryan, time is short, the avenging beast will soon be born, I shall need help, rally people of goodwill, and when the fight begins, make the Centurions' Sign together!"
