Quick Game Overview
===========================

**Deep in the heart of a fanatical land, a small band of over-equipped explorers is preparing a big heist: to unearth, unbeknownst to the population, an ancient underground temple. But time is running out.**

Will they be able to face the traps and creatures they encounter during their descent?
Will they lay their hands on the precious stones, and the archaeological treasures, that the ancient texts speak of?

"The Temple of Nazur" is an exploratory adventure-investigation RPG, strongly narrative and multimedia-enhanced, for 2 to 5 players. Total duration: 3½ h on average.



Setting Overview
==========================

On the whole, the world of Pangea is at the scientific stage of the end of our 20th century on Earth, but knowledge is monopolised by a small number of scientific centres, which guard it jealously; thus, few towns are equipped with electricity. Advanced sciences such as nuclear fission, space exploration and genetic manipulation are being theorised, but it will be decades before the industrial fabric allows them to be developed.

**The Alifir Academy**, to which the protagonists are linked, specialises in **History-Geography, Quantum Physics and Astronomy**. It can date biological material (though imprecisely) with carbon-14 dating, and is finalising a device capable of translating the obscure Akarith sacred runes.

The other cutting-edge analyses (pollen identification, DNA analysis, chemistry...) can only be carried out with the cooperation of the **foreign academies**, which would take the opportunity to obtain hefty compensation (financial or intellectual).

Various religions coexist, but the main one is **"Yodism"**, originating from Akaris, which worships an infinity of identical deities called the "Yods".


Session Scenario
==========================


Expedition background
-------------------------

**Sir Loyd Georges**, a retiree from the land of Sabarim (*Victorian England style*) who has led a not very fruitful adventurer's life, is on the verge of bankruptcy. But thanks to his connections at the Alifir Academy, he learns of the existence of an ancient dissident temple, in the **land of Akaris** (*a fanatical Yodic theocracy, with a medieval Japan atmosphere*).
He therefore mounts a last-chance expedition, to bring back the jewels and artefacts that the sanctuary probably contains, before it is found and annihilated by the local religious leaders.

Taking advantage of the population movements due to the annual pilgrimage of the Yodic faithful, Loyd Georges and his expedition infiltrate Akaris, with a **sturdy off-road truck**, and sophisticated equipment. They set up camp near the ruins of **the former heretic capital, Nazur**, a city "cursed and placed under interdict" since its fall.
Then they prepare to blow up a hillside, under cover of a **violent thunderstorm**; the locals (for whom thunder is "the Wrath of the Yods") will probably not dare to set foot outside as long as the storm lasts.
If the alarm is raised by nearby inhabitants or soldiers (the "Akarith zealots"), the idea is to get out of the country through the poorly guarded hills, outrunning the border guards.



How a session unfolds
----------------------------

The general plot is that the players descend deeper and deeper into the cave, until they find the Holy of Holies of the Yodic temple. During this exploration, they must gather as many gems and artefacts as possible, as well as clues about the history of this temple. Many heavy objects must be hauled back up as they go, on stretchers. When the storm dies down, Akariths come near the shaft blasted into the hill. Loyd Georges and the other members of the expedition must then withdraw.

If the players have already come back up, all goes well for them. Otherwise, they must find a way to escape, and to reach the truck; either by repelling the first assailants who climb down the shaft (peasants, or soldiers from the small nearby garrison), or via another exit: the canal despite the shark-snakes, or the collapsed exit tunnel, or the openings above the pagan temple, or by blowing up the sub-cavern of the Yodic temple...
A chase, inside and then outside the cavern, is possible.
All this time, the two porters are, depending on the case, either with Loyd Georges, or with the players.
The adventure ends when the players reach Loyd Georges' off-road truck (or die...).


Possible complications, in addition to those present in the description of each location:

- an explorer's corpse lies in the aquifer cavern, and additional clues or artefacts can be found on it
- a strange animal (land ray-octopus-chameleon...) discreetly follows the adventurers from the moment they enter, and once spotted can be tamed (or fought)
- one of the porters loses his mind because of the mystical atmosphere of the Holy of Holies, and all these riches (manage to calm him down)
- in the absence of a gas mask, one of the porters develops a throat oedema, as an allergic reaction to mould in the Holy of Holies (need to perform a tracheotomy on him?)



IMPORTANT - Player briefing
---------------------------------------

**Present the Universe of the planet Pangea.**

**Show the intro video** (the hill being ripped open with explosives).

**Fill in the expedition background, the local atmosphere in Nazur.**

Give the **additional instructions** below orally.

    Loyd Georges has **known you all for a long time** and has therefore entrusted you with an extremely sensitive mission; some tenuous clues suggest that a **Divine Orb**, one of the three mythical jewels of the Akarith religion, may have passed through this temple. An orb said to be luminous, **singing**, and endowed (according to legend) with monstrous powers. Keep your senses alert, for such a discovery, however geopolitically dangerous it may be, would be worth all the treasures in the world.

    A **lookout** will be left at the top of the hill, to watch the surroundings. Loyd Georges will stay at the truck with the radio operator. You will have to make a **radio check-in every 20 minutes** to report your situation.

    **Two faithful servants** of Loyd Georges (devoted men in their fifties, but not very autonomous) will accompany you into the cave, mainly to haul the finds back up **as you go**, on a stretcher (thanks to the **powerful multi-cable winch** installed at the top of the shaft); they take their orders from the butler (who may **choose their names**).

    Your team is supposed to be **very communicative**; so from the moment they are in the same room, unless instructed otherwise, the characters know what each of the others discovers *(this allows puzzles to be solved together, avoiding metagaming and absurd deadlock situations)*.

    Finally, Loyd Georges urges you to find a **fair balance between safety and efficiency**: the ranger is the best scout, and must secure the areas, but it can sometimes be useful to split up so as not to waste time, and to find as quickly as possible the Yodic temple, which is supposed to be **"deeply buried"**.

**Finally, review their equipment** (what they have on their body, on their belt, in their hand, in their backpack...), and their **default marching order**...



Game System
=================

Role assignment
----------------------

The pre-generated characters are designed to be highly complementary in their skills and missions, with a few points of friction (e.g. illegally reselling, or keeping for scientific study, the exceptional artefacts found).

Aim optimally for 3 players for a dynamic session, or 4 players for more complex interactions.

Here are the recommended characters depending on the type of session (they may be of whichever gender they wish):

- 2 players: butler and archaeologist, the ranger is an NPC under their orders
- 3 players: butler, archaeologist and ranger
- 4 players: add the naturalist to the team
- 5 players: split the archaeologist into two twins, one oriented towards "physics/geology", the other towards "ancient languages", who share skills and equipment (see the "A" and "B" marks on the character sheet)


Character attributes
-----------------------------------

The characters have attributes from 1 to 5.
You must roll equal to or under the attribute with a D6 to get a success. A 1 is an exceptional success, and a 6 is a critical failure (fumble).

These attributes should be used **very little**: most actions are supposed to be in line with the characters' expertise, and therefore **succeed automatically, unless the player fumbles**. Most die rolls are therefore there to evaluate **"luck"**, the randomness of the session.

Character attributes:

- **Constitution**: physical strength, endurance, health...
- **Agility**: speed, suppleness, fighting...
- **Observation**: detecting a danger, finding an object, noticing an odd detail...

During a (rare) fight, each action consists of an attack and an attempt at defence.
"Damage" is narrative, and depends on the type of weapon, as well as on the die score (the lower it is, the better the attack succeeds).

- ATTACK value: **Agility**
- DEFENCE value: **max(Agility - 2, 1)**


Supporting media
---------------------------


The Game Master must have:

- the slideshow containing the maps and images of the locations visited, with bestiary and objects
- the tokens representing the players, the porters, and the enemies
- the maps (or artwork) of the various game areas, printed on paper so that the tokens can be placed on them
- the image summarising the spatial layout of these locations
- the music and videos accompanying the scenario (in particular the "orb sound")
- a clock to visually show the passage of in-game time (optional)

Beware, the slideshow must be set to "manual mode" so as **not to spoil** the game's images. To display the right slideshow images (e.g. in LibreOffice Impress or MS PowerPoint), the best is to do it on a secondary screen, turned towards the players, while the Game Master views all the slides. In nomadic mode, it is possible to project the slideshow onto a touch tablet via USB or Wifi, with software such as *TwomonUSB* or *Splashtop Xdisplay*.

**Tip**: the "Quick Reference" section of this manual can be stuck to the back of the GM screen, so as to be accessible at all times.



Locations and Possible Actions
=================================


Gameplay Common to the Whole Game
-----------------------------------

- the players must orient themselves in space, to guess where the various passages may lead in relation to the outside geography, and find shortcuts.
- they must pay attention to their positioning in the cavern, and their marching order, which can be decisive in the event of a sudden attack.
- they must remember to adapt their equipment (gas mask, lighting or night-vision devices...) before running into trouble.
- the butler must make the best use of the porters' round trips; but he must remember to take into account the ranger's safety advice, and the archaeologist's recommendations (e.g. some artefacts must be kept on site to solve puzzles).
- some events and details are left to the GM's discretion, with suggested probabilities if the dice are to decide.



The Hill Outside
--------------------

- A porter asks the archaeologist how long the storm will last. Loyd Georges has no idea.
- the players must remember to study the storm intuitively [ranger] or by analysing the weather reports (pressure, precipitation, winds...) [archaeologist], to see that the storm will last **2 hours at most**.
- ask the players whether they have any questions for Loyd Georges or for the **lookout**, while they set up the **winch**.


Aquifer Cavern
--------------------

- a slightly damp cavern, with a few stalactites/stalagmites
- **100 m long**, gently sloping from the arrival point
- under the rubble caused by the blasting of the shaft, one can make out a **cut-stone platform**, surrounded by a few steps, which the archaeologist could recognise as the forecourt of a **pre-Yodic processional tunnel**; it is possible to clear this rubble with explosives, to open a shortcut to the base camp.
- large, very old graffiti on the various walls, curses fairly typical of the Akariths: **"Heretics, turn back before the Yods drown you in fire"**, **"Suffering and death to the impious"**...
- **pinkish mushrooms** grow on the mounds to the north-west; they have mutated compared to their white, edible counterparts on the surface, and are therefore **mild sedatives**.
- in the middle of the mounds is an **old wooden chest**; it contains a **silver chalice**, connected to a firing mechanism **(1 kilo of black powder)**; it can be defused by ripping open the side of the chest.
- **blue water mushrooms** grow in the crevices to the south-west; they are an **unknown species** (but edible)
- the canal lies below the rest of the cavern, bordered by **3 m of steep slope** (but climbable); two conduits open into it from the east, below the surface (the sluice system described below can be explored from there by diving).
- the canal is full of **Chlamydes, or shark-snakes**, but they are afraid of moving lights; being amphibious, they can slither out of the canal to hunt; they have a nest (holes in the rock) on the north face of the canal.
- the **bridge is covered in pitch**; the slightest spark sets off **10 minutes** of fire and smoke, blocking access, and alerting the surrounding countryside; an old half-buried fuse leads away from the pitch, and runs off towards the north-east.
- the **boat wreckage and pierced coins**, near the canal, are on a ledge **1.5 m above the water**; this was bait, intended to blow up intruders thanks to the adjoining **keg of black powder** (with the same fuse system as for the bridge); the powder (1 kilo) may be dry (2-in-3 chance).
- a **huge monolith** in precarious balance (a stalagmite whose base has been worn away by trickling water) stands in the centre-east of the cavern (it can be used to crush an enemy in case of a fight).
- the wide eastern corridor is **entirely flooded over 10 m**, because of the conduits (a wide one to the east, two narrower ones to the west) which respectively fill it with and drain it of its water (see the diagram).
- in the north-east corner are **4 large levers** arranged in a square, initially vertical. They correspond to the archaeologist's riddle of the "beings of the air and of the earth". Each must be tipped towards its opening cardinal point, so that more water can flow out of the flooded corridor. In the non-flooded part of the corridor, to the east (off the map), are the **2 other sluice gates**, which must be left as they are, or each tipped towards its closing cardinal point (to speed up the draining of the corridor).
- **2 wooden plungers** are located in the centre-east of the cavern; they do not control sluice gates, but each triggers the firing of one of the fuses (powder keg or pitch-covered bridge), if pushed in.
- a **large lone lever** is located in the south-east of the cavern, near the canal outlet; it bears a fish inscription, and is initially tilted towards the south. If tipped the other way, this closes the outlet, and the water gradually rises in the cavern (first submerging the ledge with the boat wreckage).

- possible paths:

    - the tunnel to the north-west leads towards the pagan temple (but with partial rubble)
    - the tunnel to the north-east leads to the outside (but with heavy rubble)
    - the corridor to the east forms a dip (initially flooded), then descends towards the Yodic temple cavern
    - the main canal becomes an underground duct (airless) then, after 50 m, exits the hill as a stream, and flows into the River Nazuron



Pagan Temple
--------------

- the tunnel leading to it is **collapsed** towards the end

- ancient Akarith graffiti on the fallen stones: **"Thus ends every cult devoted to the vile idols"**

- **very faint light** filters in at the top of the collapse (only the ranger sees it); a passage can be opened simply with an ice axe, leading into the pagan temple

- a fairly dry cavity, less cool than the aquifer cavern, and dimly lit by wide but tortuous fissures in the ceiling

- archaeologist's intuition: ancient Akariths blew up this pagan temple, but the pediment remained standing, although the tunnel leading to it collapsed

- the fissures in the ceiling make it possible to reach the north flank of the hill, after **15 m** of climbing

- if the distance to the surface is determined: the **tree roots**, visible in the ceiling, prove that these common trees of Akaris go looking for water at **exceptional depths**

- the **nest of a forest yeti** (not recognisable), made of still-green branches, is set up behind the columns; it is an **unknown species**, mentioned only in local legends

- **1-in-3** chance that the yeti is in its nest and attacks the players

- [archaeologist] study the pediment to realise that this is a pagan temple far predating Yodism, magnificently preserved, but too "cursed" to have later been able to shelter Yodic dissidents

- [butler] **3 still-intact pagan statues** can be resold at a good price

- **1-in-3** chance that the yeti comes back, soaking wet, if the players study the statues, or widen the passage to bring them back



Tunnel to the Outside
------------------------

- at the start of the tunnel, a **torch rack** holds a few wooden torches (a sign that people were arriving from a lit area).

- the tunnel leads to the north face of the hill, but is **collapsed** in the middle, naturally

- [archaeologist] using the sonar: there are about **3 m of rock** blocking the way

- [butler] then knows that the equivalent of **2 sticks of dynamite** is needed to open the passage, otherwise it will take a long time to finish with a pickaxe



Yodic Temple Cavern
---------------------------

- a fairly dry cavity, but cooler than the aquifer cavern
- a few tree roots visible on the vault
- building in good condition, solid columns, thick timber frame, but unstable tiles on the roof, and windows blocked up with cob
- the archaeologist sees that this is architecture typical of the temples of a civilisation that died out a thousand years ago
- the archaeologist knows that the recess beside the temple is just an old **plinth for ritual fires**
- at the **top of the entrance portico**, there is a niche, in which a religious insignia is usually found; a **crossentar** can be retrieved, proving that Yodic faithful laid claim to this pre-Yodic place
- high, dark fissures on the wall facing the temple; they shelter **amphibious luminescent bats**, an **unknown species**, which flee if disturbed (they leave the cavern via the underground canal; their luminous bellies frighten the shark-snakes); **brown guano** can be collected from the floor of the fissures, of high market value because it is radioactive (besides the radiation detector, the mass spectrometer can reveal this, by detecting unusual atoms)


Main Hall of the Pre-Yodic Temple
---------------------------------------------

**The central ark** is empty, but typical of a monotheistic religion predating Yodism; the Akariths consider it a respectable, but now obsolete, precursor. It is possible to try to detach and bring back the ark, but it is long and difficult (requires **4 porters** minimum).

Tree roots are visible in the ceiling, as in the pagan temple (**8 m** to the surface). Dynamiting the ceiling of this hall is a (very risky) possibility if the players are cornered in the cavern.

In the small crypt of the Yodic temple (which opens to the right of the ark), a huge (and extremely rare) **imperial monitor** sleeps; it is a **giant, venomous lizard**, usually timid, except when its lair is threatened; it has a poor sense of smell, sees in the dark, but only detects movement. The players can ignore it, or try to put it to sleep, or block the exit of its lair... but if they ignore it, it will eventually wake up, and intercept passers-by. This monitor usually goes through the Chlamydes' canal to leave the cavern.

By sounding the **rear gallery** (between the columns and the wall), the players may notice that this is not an original partition; it is much darker and more crumbly (to break through it: ice axe or explosives).

A **trap (spring-loaded spikes)** is triggered if one walks along the rear gallery, stepping on **one of the flagstones**; it inflicts a serious wound to the thigh if the character is hit.

A **large sliding metal hatch** is also visible at floor level, on the right-hand side: it is not big enough to get the large artefacts of the place through.




Holy of Holies of the Yodic Temple
----------------------------------------


- **trap**: put on the **survival mask** when entering the Holy of Holies (smell of mould and putrefaction), otherwise risk of lung infection after several days, due to infectious mould
- it takes a little time for the **oxygen to return** (the torches go out at first)
- the Holy of Holies is rather dry, but very cold, at 2-3°C, probably because of the surrounding water table
- main elements from left to right: **standing gravestones, a warrior statue, a heap of objects, a well, a wall fresco, a small pagoda, a low table**
- **important: a curled-up corpse**, hidden by the warrior statue

- a porter suggests placing **mini-geodes** everywhere
- disturbing detail: **traces of vandalism**, of blows and cuts, on the wall decorations and on many pieces of furniture, when they are examined; the GM can pass this off as enormous claw marks; *this is a teaser puzzle, solved in a later game*
- if the players think of looking up, there are **Akarith sacred runes** randomly arranged on the ceiling, on the well side

- **basic gems** are scattered throughout the room, under the dust
- **fine diamonds** are inlaid in the wall fresco
- the players must analyse these precious stones to see whether they are real, and which ones are worth picking up first (the **purple gems are fake**, worthless)

- if the ground in front of the standing gravestones is searched: nothing special to dig up, because in this religion the dead are cremated and then their ashes are placed directly in the earth
- **large lichens** cover the gravestones: they are what generates the **toxic gas**; under the lichens, traces of paint, almost erased (to attempt to restore, to get the **dates of death** later than the fall of Nazur)
- the gravestones can be torn out and brought back, to be sold
- the warrior statue bears a name ("Bartzar"), and **sacred runes**, which will therefore only be decipherable by the Alifir Academy
-  the **corpse** (behind the warrior statue) is naturally mummified; it wears an earth-coloured toga (very widespread in this country); if searched, around its neck it has a **gold vial** (containing a little Berserk Elixir) and a **brass key**

- in the heap of objects, the players find a number of interesting elements: **2 skulls (which the ranger can recognise as goat and dog), an empty small keg, a rusty boat propeller, pottery, a liturgical candle, and a demijohn with a viscous deposit at the bottom**

  - the dog skull and the small keg are of no interest
  - the goat skull has **filed-down horns**
  - the demijohn has a deposit of **red plum liqueur** at the bottom
  - the propeller, the pottery and the candle have archaeological and market value

- the semicircular well is covered by a **heavy iron grate**, rusty but sophisticated; **"Filter of Orthodoxy"** is engraved on the grate, in Old Akarith
- the well is very shallow (20 cm of water at the bottom), with a thin layer of silt at the bottom
- a few small **pearly fish**, which have become blind and translucent over the generations, eke out a living in this water
- at the bottom of the well, under the silt, there is a **halberd** (very blunt; **if it is an exceptional artefact, it bears Razulite inscriptions**), as well as a **hexagonal recess about 15 cm in diameter**

- the fresco is extremely fine, and contains Yodic inscriptions and symbols
- on a flat surface below, there are traces of pigmentation; by restoring this painting, one can read: **"When the Glow of Faith passes through the Sieve of Doctrine, the Glory of the Yods is revealed"** *(reference to the well puzzle)*
- on one side is engraved the **Symbol of Strength**, with pictograms inside it of a **goat, a mushroom, a round fruit, and water** *(clue for the Berserk Elixir)*

- the small dried-clay pagoda is locked; if one tries to force it with an ice axe, there is a high risk of it collapsing and damaging its contents
- the **pagoda key** is indeed the one around the corpse's neck
- in the pagoda, one can find **rolled scrolls (Yodic holy books, potentially a version specific to the Razulites), and a stone lantern** (if inspected, **"Light of the Faithful"** is engraved on it, in Old Akarith)

- the small furniture of the room (low table...) is presumably worthless

**The Light Puzzle**: if the stone lantern is wedged into the hollow at the bottom of the well, any light source is placed in it, and the grate is then put on top, the interference effects highlight certain sacred runes on the ceiling, which actually form a **Razulite prophecy dating from after the fall of Nazur**; this prophecy will only be decipherable by the Alifir Academy, but at the very least the ideogram of the **Divine Orbs** can be recognised in it.

After a while (sooner, if mini-geodes have been placed everywhere), a **strange whistling** is heard (see dedicated soundtrack): the **orb's song**. Its source is very hard to locate by ear. The walls must be sounded with the sonar, or the microphone kit used, to find out which wall to dig into with an ice axe. The Orb sits in plain view in its hiding place, resting on a **gold ingot**. It reacts violently (light and vibration) when someone tries to take it. It can be put in ice-cold water to calm it down and carry it away without it attracting Akarith zealots.
Possible locations of the hiding place: behind the small pagoda, or behind the wall fresco, or under the floor in the middle of the room...

Quick Reference
===================

Sample timeline
---------------------------------

- 0: the hill is breached by the explosion
- 5: arrival on the floor of the aquifer cavern
- 70: if the players are in the Holy of Holies of the temple, they hear the humming of the orb
- 90: if the imperial monitor has not been dealt with, it wakes up and intercepts the players or porters
- 105: the Akariths arrive if pitch smoke or smoke grenades have been released
- 120: the Akariths arrive in any case

**Time costs**:

- most actions take **5 min**
- very short actions (e.g. asking another character for information they know) are not counted
- long actions (e.g. bringing a heavy artefact back to camp) have specific time costs
- if the players palaver for too long before deciding on the next step of their expedition, 5 min go by...


Resaleable or Exceptional Finds
--------------------------------------------

In case the butler does not gather enough loot: the **exceptional artefacts** can be sold **for 20 to 30 kashes** on the black market, but this is a big risk of scandal (and legal trouble), as well as heartbreak for the scientists.
A poor stopgap: the players can agree to first have the exceptional artefact analysed by the Alifir Academy, then fake its theft to pay off Loyd Georges' debts.

The **wild beasts** (yeti, Chlamydes, monitor...) can be captured and resold **for 10 to 20 kashes** to a zoo, but the naturalist will have to be convinced.

- silver chalice: 5 kashes
- pierced coins: 5 kashes
- 3 pagan statues: 15 kashes
- brown guano (radioactive): 1/2 kash per kilo, 50 kilos collectable at a rate of 1 kilo per person per minute
- stone ark of the Yodic temple: exceptional artefact
- Akarith warrior statue: exceptional artefact
- crossentar: 10 kashes (unless exceptional artefact, 1-in-3 chance)
- halberd: 10 kashes (unless exceptional artefact, 1-in-3 chance)
- rusty grate: 10 kashes
- stone lantern: 5 kashes
- sacred scrolls: 10 kashes
- heaped objects: 10 kashes for the lot
- 5 gravestones: 10 kashes for the lot
- one kilo of real loose gems is worth 10 kashes, and is gathered in 5 min; up to 6 kilos can be picked up, but among them, 2 kilos of purple gems are **worthless**
- one kilo of inlaid diamonds is worth 15 kashes, and is gathered in 15 min; 1 kilo can be collected
- gold ingot: 10 kashes
- Divine Tear: super-mega-exceptional artefact, unsellable

**Other items**: **black powder** can be collected in the cavern; 1 kilo of thoroughly dry black powder is equivalent to a stick of dynamite; **old slow fuse** can also be found, burning at 1 m/s


Hauling Back the Finds
-----------------------------------------

The butler must remember to have the heavy objects hauled up by the porters as soon as possible, in case their exploration is interrupted by the arrival of the locals.

A **round trip** from the Holy of Holies to the base camp takes **20 min**, if not too heavily loaded.
So less time if the players are still only in the main cavern, or if it is just their final return to the base camp.
The best trick remains to find a closer exit, and to bring the truck up at the last moment.

**Example hauls**, with 2 porters together:

- 3 pagan statues
- 5 gravestones
- Akarith warrior statue
- rusty grate (heavy)
- the rest: bulky objects from the heap, crossentar, sacred scrolls, stone lantern, halberd...

**The stone ark of the Yodic temple** requires twice as many porters and twice as much time to be brought back

Small items (gems and ingots, Divine Tear...) are carried in the backpacks, without being a hindrance.


Naturalist investigation
-------------------------

Summary of the new species to "capture" (on photo/video, or in the flesh): **blue water mushrooms, forest yeti, amphibious luminescent bats, blind well-fish**

Summary of the new scientific facts:

- the pinkish ground mushrooms (which are white when in the open air) produce a sedative, in their lightless metabolism
- the imperial monitor coexists with the Chlamydes (the underground canal is the only possible way out for it)
- the lichens on the gravestones produce a toxic gas when deprived of light
- the pearly fish of the well have lost their eyes and their colouring over time


Archaeological investigation
----------------------------

The archaeologist's goal is to demonstrate that the Yodic temple, and the artefacts brought back, are an "archaeological scoop"; that they bring new evidence about the end of the "Razulite" Yodic denomination, which was crushed by its "Taranite" rival, some **500 years** ago.

The problem is that the inhabitants of the place deliberately kept a low profile, so as to have a chance of getting away if they were discovered. Most of the objects that can be unearthed are therefore admittedly old - even predating the era of the Yodic conflicts - but relatively ordinary and widespread. Moreover, the distinctions between the Yodic denominations hinge on minute theological details, which complicates the investigation.

Consequently, the archaeologist will have to redouble his efforts to substantiate the various scientific theses.

To prove that it is a Yodic temple:

- find the crossentar (the Yodic symbol par excellence)
- find the rolled scrolls (Yodic holy books, common to all denominations)
- photograph the wall fresco of the Holy of Holies, typically Yodic

To prove that it belongs to the Razulite denomination:

- solve the puzzle of the lantern in the well, which picks out a message in Akarith sacred runes on the vault
- if the halberd is an exceptional artefact, its inscriptions include Razulite mottoes
- if the statue of the warrior Bartzar is brought back, 1-in-3 chance that this eventually makes it possible to establish the link with the Razulite denomination

To prove that it was used after the fall of Nazur:

- the runic message on the ceiling (puzzle of the lantern in the well) speaks of the fall of Nazur
- 1-in-6 chance that the analysis of the corpse gives conclusive results (carbon-14 dating is imprecise)
- 1-in-6 chance that the micro-inscriptions of the wall fresco give calendar references
- by restoring the paintings on the gravestones (1-in-2 chance that it works), one can see that the dates of death are later than the fall of Nazur

**Historical summary**: a monotheistic church had long existed at the back of the cavern, hence the western corridor and the underground bridge. The church was forgotten in favour of a neo-pagan temple. The Razulites of Nazur then dynamited the pagan temple, and "trimmed" the church to make it compatible. Then, hunted by the Taranites, they reoccupied the church, filling in the western entrance and creating a discreet new passage (in the north-east of the aquifer cavern).


The Berserk Elixir Quest
----------------------------

The ranger is looking for the recipe of this legendary potion, the secret of which the Razulites took with them in their downfall.
For several hours, it increases strength tenfold, and makes one insensitive to pain.

The corpse has a little of it, in the gold vial attached to its neck; but there is little chance that this allows the recipe to be reconstituted (the mass spectrometer only gives the atomic elements, not the molecules, and the Alifir Academy is very poor at biochemistry).

The wall fresco of the Holy of Holies gives the recipe for this potion, with pictograms of a **goat, a mushroom, a round fruit, and water**

- the mushrooms are those that grow in the water of the crevices of the aquifer cavern (not those in the open air)
- the goat skull has filed-down horns; it is this powder that serves as an ingredient
- the demijohn has a deposit of red plum liqueur at the bottom

Mixing these three elements gives the Berserk Elixir.

If he has not determined the exact ingredients, but has noted these pictograms, the ranger has some chance of still finding the right recipe eventually, by **trial and error**.

If he has brought the mass spectrometer into play, he also finds the right dose of each ingredient to use to maximise the effect, and thus obtain **double potency**.


Possible Fights
-----------------------

Fights are very rare; the idea is that the players only face opponents if they are theoretically sure to win.

Initiative is either determined by the scenario (surprise), or by a die roll with a 1-in-2 chance.

Agility (= attack) of the main enemies:

- Forest yeti: 3
- Chlamyde in the water: 3
- Chlamyde out of the water: 1
- Imperial monitor: 4
- Akarith peasant: 2 (with a scythe)
- Akarith zealot: 3 (with a musket then a katana)
