

Running the evening
===========================


Launching the evening
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

The bidders are invited to come well dressed, for the Opening Gala of Loyd Georges' Auctions. At the entrance, they go through a **body scanner**, and are informed that this certifies that none of them is carrying blades, firearms, or explosive substances (not even the small synthetic weapons fashionable among secret agents). {% fact 'shark_enforces_body_scanner_at_manor_entrance' as author %}

Shark has also set up an **electromagnetic signal jammer**, so the guests' mobile phones can no longer make/receive "normal" calls (but WIFI is not affected). {% fact 'shark_blocks_mobile_network_around_manor' as author %}

The guests begin to enjoy aperitifs and chat, while awaiting the arrival of their host. It is also an opportunity to get better acquainted. Musical ambience: classical, harpsichord, nobility and gaiety... even if the servants seem strangely gloomy.

Then Inspector Shark arrives; change of mood: oppressive music (or no music at all). The inspector then briefly introduces himself, as the investigator in charge of the threats/thefts of which Loyd Georges has been the target.

He then announces, in a grave and menacing tone, that Loyd Georges has been attacked, that he is currently hovering between life and death, and that all the guests are presumed guilty until the case has been solved. To cover his back, Shark may refrain from specifying the details of the murder attempt, or else throw out false leads ("a brawl in his bedroom", "strangulation marks on his neck"...).

He warns that the manor and grounds are full of police officers (which is true), and that he is going to put each of the guests (who, he can feel, have plenty of offences to their name) to work solving this crime. His real objective is of course to find the trail of the orbs, starting with the one Loyd Georges brought back from Akaris.

The inspector then begins a series of intense interrogations with each guest (or sometimes, pairs of guests). His aim is to obtain, step by step, more and more information not only about their recent whereabouts (which he already partly knows), but also about their past misdeeds, and their links with the secret services of the various states.

It is therefore a game of bluff for the inspector, during which he must try to extract, under the pretext of a criminal investigation, as much information as possible from the guests, while delaying by all means their progress towards the truth about this murder. And the more compromising information he obtains from someone, the more pressure he can put on the others (up to and including "indictments"), and thus obtain clues for his true mission of fanatical espionage.

Meanwhile, the players interact with each other, question the non-player characters, ask them for services, analyse the clues they glean by a multitude of means, and advance their own personal or state objectives.

The evening is thus launched, and it will be punctuated by multiple incidents, false leads, scandals, and manipulations, until its conclusion.




Example of the inspector's introductory speech
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

*The inspector's entry into the game is crucial: the players must understand that their initial missions have been turned upside down, that they must now protect themselves against any testimony and material evidence against them, and in particular prevent their secrets from being searched out.*

    Ladies and Gentlemen, good evening. Loyd Georges would have liked to welcome you. Unfortunately, he will not do so tonight. He will not do so, because you do not deserve it. He will not do so... because he is dying. Tonight it is I, **Inspector Shark of the Sabarim police**, who will be your host.

    Loyd Georges is one of the most eminent citizens of Sabarim, a man whose perseverance and courage will forever inspire the new generations. One of you has committed the irreparable by attacking his person, and will leave here in handcuffs, to meet a punishment of rare severity.

    Make no mistake, I know that you **all have dirty hands**, and in other circumstances I would have taken pleasure in leading you one by one to your downfall. But I am willing to trade my **leniency for your cooperation**.

    Granted, we live under the **Rule of Law**. You are currently merely **"suspects requisitioned for testimony"**. But it will take me only a few pieces of evidence to **indict** you, and have both **your pockets and your digital accounts on Anthropia** searched. If you do not want to be compromised, both here in Sabarim and in your respective "homelands", you know what you have to do.

    The manor and grounds are swarming with police officers armed to the teeth, and it is now up to justice to investigate. I will receive you in the study, for questioning, and you will not be able to leave here until I have cornered the criminal or criminals hiding among you.

    In the meantime, refreshments are served; carry on with your conversations, but **do not expect to be able to use your mobile phones, I have installed a telephone signal jammer so that tonight we remain, as far as possible, among dinner guests.**




The inspector's interrogation mechanics
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++

Here are the offences the secret agents are generally guilty of, so many levers for the inspector to put them "under pressure":

- Identity theft (this is the case for all players who are in fact secret agents)
- Collusion with foreign secret services, endangering state security
- Interception of postal communications or private conversations

Other misdeeds are far more specific, so the investigator must fight to obtain information before he can accuse a player of them:

- Attempted assault on Loyd Georges (as soon as there are "beginnings of evidence" to that effect)
- Hacking (or attempted hacking) of Anthropia accounts
- Theft at Loyd Georges' manor, or at the Alifir Academy...
- Hold-up of the Bank of Salima...
- Smuggling, tax fraud...
- Blackmail, threats...

**Psychological games the inspector can put into play:**

- Start of course with the unavoidable questions: **their alibi for the previous evening**, the reasons for their presence at these auctions...
- Vary postures, expressions and tone from one moment to the next, thus making yourself psychologically "elusive" to the group of players. Perhaps go as far as talking to yourself, or breaking into a maniacal snigger, when you are among the guests outside interrogations.
- Suddenly become very friendly with a player who brings information such as an easily verifiable alibi, or elements against another protagonist...
- Hint "I don't think it's you, but you're going to help me investigate <so-and-so>, I have strong suspicions about him because of <this>"; pit the players against one another, even within the same camp.
- Say: "despite my presence, plenty of dramas have befallen LG, and now his very life has been attacked; the honour of a whole lifetime of service is at stake, I will settle for nothing less than total victory".
- Test the players on their knowledge of antiquities or precious stones, according to their official identity (look for trick questions in the dedicated sheets or on the Internet): thus show the secret agents that you are not fooled by their impostures.
- Say: "I know you are an agent of the government of <country>. I'll hand you over to the Sabarith secret services if you don't give me a piece of information."
- Say: "I have the media and the State on my back, so I want a culprit, whoever it may be, and I'll turn you over to the government if you don't cooperate in finding one (real or fake)."
- Go after, later on, the players who lacked ingenuity in their alibis or statements during an interrogation.
- Confront players who might have information on each other, to blackmail them "prisoner's dilemma" style ("the first one to talk will have my favour, too bad for the other").
- Stage a "good cop / bad cop" act with your deputy police officer to push those being questioned to seek support from the "good cop"
- Take advantage of having cornered a player to turn to their partner (e.g. for those who came together to LG's interview)
- Towards the players concerned by the following facts, the inspector can bluff: "you brought Loyd Georges some <food/drink> as a gift; we found traces of <poison> in it!". The player will then be able to clear themselves either by demanding the certified forensic analysis report, or by proving that another player drank some (but is that player on the same team as the first, making them suspect too?), or by requesting a duplicate receipt from the seller, via one of LG's valets (the bottle being sealed with wax, it is then hardly suspect; as for the nuts, they had their shells, so hard to poison them...)
- Towards the player who had an altercation with LG during his interview: "I was able to follow your interview with Loyd Georges yesterday from a distance, let's face it, it was stormy... would you be the kind of man to let yourself be carried away by your urges for revenge?"
- Towards the player who retraced his steps, the inspector can bluff: "The butler saw you listening at the door of LG's study. What exactly were you trying to find out?" (A clever player will probably deny everything outright, or talk about industrial/commercial espionage to justify himself)
- Bargaining: "I know what you did, <this> and <that>, give me more important information than that, or I'll make you go down" (then have them inform on their colleague, or another faction...)
- If there is incriminating evidence of guilt in the poisoning of Loyd Georges, transition to Shark's true quest: "LG risked his life to obtain that orb, give it back (that one, or another one for that matter, as I know there are several in play), and you will be treated with magnanimity". The inspector thereby reveals a little that he is pursuing the orbs more than the culprit of the crime.
- If the opportunity arises: hand a player an off-hook telephone; on the other end of the line is either a relative of the cover identity, or the real person whose identity the player has taken. The imposture is thus unmasked in front of everyone.
- To a compromised player, the inspector can either offer to betray their camp and be entitled to a fresh start in Sabarim (which is a bluff, Shark does not have such prerogatives), or (if he senses an affinity for it, e.g. with the Dorians if the Akariths announce an alliance to that effect) reveal a little of his Akarith Creed, and offer to join the "Chosen of the Yods" in the new era that is beginning.


**BEWARE: as a general rule, you have depositions signed, and issue indictment requests, only using the typewriter, so as not to risk being betrayed by your handwriting.**



Other suggested behaviour during the evening
++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++++


If the Tabellion, servants, or guests remind you of the law, you will have to take it into account, but you will not hide your irritation and resentment. You will be particularly aggressive towards the Tabellion, who enjoys judicial immunity, and interferes with your plans.

You show great restraint towards the Akarith Ambassador, and accept his privileges far more readily than the Tabellion's, saying that "that's a matter of foreign affairs, not internal affairs".

If the Sabarith anthem is played on the radio, you will watch the guests, so as to accuse of insulting the anthem anyone who dares to snigger or talk while the Sabariths present all stand to attention.

You are the type to verbally assault any protagonist who might behave "suspiciously": wearing gloves, making a phone call, etc.; this tyranny will throw the players off balance.

Expected behaviour with the servants: the inspector is at first "very professional" with them, but when they start helping the players in their own investigations, he quickly gets irritated, to the point of uttering strange Akarith religious interjections... and he does not fail to lose his temper if the old servant Alphonse, through his blunders, hampers his manoeuvres.

You have a document, the "Elements of Sabarith Law", which you keep with you, even though you are ready to try to circumvent its provisions for the needs of this very special investigation.
