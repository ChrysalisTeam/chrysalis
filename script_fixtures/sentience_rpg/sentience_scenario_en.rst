Chrysalis: Sentience - The Alchemist Is Dead
##############################################


**Common abilities of the "sapient" animals:**

- communicate telepathically with the other "sapient" animals (range: 5 km)
- understand the spoken language of humans
- communicate rudimentarily with the (non-"sapient") members of their OWN species

°

**Marmoset**

- perceived as "super cute" by humans
- climbs trees and leaps between branches
- prehensile hands
- reads and writes human language
- juggles with small objects
- swims underwater
- polyglot: also communicates rudimentarily with the OTHER animal species

°

**Owl**

- perceived as an ill omen, when encountered in daytime
- silent flight (when not carrying anyone)
- sees in the dark (night vision)
- keen eye and sharp talons
- speaks human language
- has observed the ways of the village humans a little
- inception: knows how to insert an idea, a desire, a topic of conversation, into a human's head

°

**Wombat (burrowing marsupial)**

- perceived as vermin by humans
- digs tunnels and squeezes through
- keen hearing and keen sense of smell
- septic saliva
- belly pouch for storage
- cartilaginous rump shield
- psychologist: knows how to gauge the authenticity of a human reaction

°

**Ancestral Tortoise (NPC)**

- radiating telepathy (range 50 km)
- reminiscence: can relive the last emotionally charged scene experienced by an artefact
- other potential powers, but little known

Synopsis
================

The kind alchemist, hermit of the forest, is dead. Or rather, has been murdered by a human.

You were her closest friends; small forest animals that had become "sapient", that is to say, to whom she had given intelligence as well as various powers.

So for you begins a quest for justice, or even revenge.

*Scenario created from the idea "The witch is dead", from the one-page RPGs*

Very simple game system: 1 six-sided die, rolls of variable difficulty depending on the situation; a "1" (critical failure) is only really dangerous on risky actions.


Prologue - read in full to the players
=========================================

The alchemist **Séléné**, disappointed by the behaviour of humans, lived further and further away from her village, called **Sylvorann**.

Worried by the humans' growing covetousness towards the resources of the forest, twelve years ago she negotiated a **Blood Pact** between herself and her home village. The humans jointly undertook not to touch the trees, the stones, and the other riches of the forest, and to hunt or gather only what was strictly necessary for them to eat and clothe themselves; in return, the beasts and monsters of the forest undertook, through Séléné, never to spill out of their territory, even in times of famine, and never to attack humans (except in self-defence). This pact was of course backed by magical guarantees: luck and prosperity as long as it was respected, misfortune and catastrophes otherwise.

Since then, Séléné has pursued her research in the alchemical arts, directing it towards animals.
She has achieved wonders in this field, with potions capable of making certain forest beasts "sapient", that is to say endowed with reason and inventiveness. These successes earned her a good share of the prizes at the **last three Triennial Congresses of Wonders** - which naturally aroused some jealousy.
The **Ancestral Tortoise**, her familiar, became her research assistant. And Séléné continued her work by awakening to intelligence a marmoset, an owl, and lately a wombat.

Over time, Séléné was increasingly absorbed by her work. She still, rarely, happened to receive visits from a few notables of the village. Or from her friends: **the Enchantress Dorina** (whose familiar is an extremely rare **green-billed toucan**), and the **Druid Galien** (whose familiar is a **dwarf bear**); both of them magicians living at the other ends of the forest.

It is in this state of affairs that our story begins.


The Secret Behind the Affair (GM only)
================================================================

For some years now, the industrialisation of the world has been accelerating, and among the newly prized resources are rock oil, but also (yellowish) **sulphur crystals**, which are often mined in bluish-coloured rock massifs. One of these massifs lies in Séléné's forest.

**Honoré des Hautvents**, burgomaster of the village for 30 years, is beginning to regret the pact he made with the forest. He could become rich by letting a corporation such as the **Rockies Company** exploit the mineral wealth of the place. He therefore begins to discuss, with his close circle, a way of getting rid of the alchemist Séléné, and thus putting an end to the Blood Pact.

This close circle is made up of 2 people: **Fénelon, the notary** of the village and partner in all dirty tricks. And **Joséphine des Hautvents, his only daughter**, who came back to the village to recharge her batteries a few months ago, after a fine start to her career in the administration of a railway transport company.

One fine day, an explosion rings out in the forest, and rocky debris lands on the village; a certain **Émile the tanner** thus ends up buried under a rock, in his house. Other dwellings are hit, forcing their inhabitants to **relocate temporarily to the inn**. Hypotheses abound about the origin of this catastrophe. Joséphine then pushes her father to see his ideas through: to write a letter purportedly signed by the alchemist herself, and claiming this rain of rock as a curse intended to drive the "stinking hairless apes" (meaning the humans) out of the land.

Since the alchemist only writes her rare missives with a very specific **typewriter**, Joséphine advises her father to take the plot even further: to write this forged letter imitating the handwriting of **the Enchantress Dorina** (of which Joséphine has obtained a sample), and to claim that this letter was dropped by an extremely rare **green-billed toucan** - known to be the Enchantress's familiar. Thus, in the event of a future investigation, it is the Enchantress who would find herself accused of conspiracy; all the more so as she has a perfect motive, having often been Séléné's **unsuccessful rival** at the famous Triennial Congress of Wonders.

Once the letter has been skilfully forged by Fénelon the notary, the plot unfolds as planned: the burgomaster claims to have received the letter from a green-billed toucan, stirs up the grieving village against Séléné, then dispatches his daughter to the big city to fetch a specialist in the fight against black magic.

10 days later, Joséphine returns with **Abélard the witcher**, whose many exploits she had supposedly heard tell of.
After a single day spent in the village, the witcher sets off with his black stallion, his light armour, and his combat gear, towards the centre of the forest.

There, Abélard breaks down the door of Séléné's tower, and murders her. This is the moment when the story of the game begins.

---

**The Real Truth of the Matter**: in reality, Joséphine des Hautvents hates her authoritarian, emasculating father, who never believed in her, and even tried to marry her off by force before she fled to the big city. Unsurprisingly, when she comes back home and brings up the current rush for sulphur crystals, the burgomaster appropriates this idea, and she cannot play her role as business introducer for the **Minéralys Company**, which has designs on the region's deposits.

She therefore guides the Minéralys Company, by telegraph, so that one of their teams secretly assesses the quality of the rock seams. But these diggings trigger the curse of the Blood Pact: the **3 Minéralys explorers** set off a (rare) sulphur explosion, which brings rocks down on the village, and none of them returns.

The alchemist Séléné notices the intrusion, decently **buries on the spot** what little remains of the explorers, and retrieves their few **papers and logbooks** with a view to returning them to the village. She sends an angry letter to the village burgomaster, asking him to crack down so that these illicit explorations cease immediately.
The alchemist has this missive delivered to the letterbox by her **messenger raven**, but it is intercepted and concealed by Joséphine, who does not want her mining schemes to be discovered.

The burgomaster's daughter immediately seizes on this rockfall catastrophe by encouraging her father to eliminate Séléné (who remains an obstacle for everyone, among other things as guardian of the Blood Pact), while setting him up to slip up: the sample of the Enchantress Dorina's handwriting that she provides to her father is **deliberately fake**, and she knows full well that the Enchantress's green-billed toucan **died more than a year ago**.

In the process, Joséphine brings back from the city a faithful ally from the start of her career, the witcher Abélard. He has a well-oiled plan to follow:

- kill the alchemist, retrieve the compromising writings, but leave the rest in place
- put himself forward as a potential successor to the burgomaster, and thus trigger a split in the village
- help the villagers discover the burgomaster's manipulation (e.g. the alchemist's manuscripts, then information about the Enchantress Dorina), or if necessary have him accused of "attempted murder out of jealousy" against his own person as a witcher
- have the burgomaster imprisoned or banished from the village
- set himself up as a sham electoral opponent against Joséphine, while leaving - in any case - the way clear for the Minéralys Company to exploit the seams of the forest.

How the game unfolds
========================

0 - Prelude
------------------

The Ancestral Tortoise, a close friend of Séléné, calls through her powerful telepathic aura upon the beasts of the forest to come and mourn: the alchemist was murdered while reading in her bed. She has a crossbow bolt planted in her heart. Her head, with its **long red hair**, has disappeared.

Many are the animals, tamed by the alchemist, that come running.
In particular the 3 other sapients - the owl, the marmoset and the wombat.

The Tortoise, distraught, orders them to go and bring justice, and to teach the humans a lesson, by eliminating the murderous mercenary.

For her part, she takes care of preparing a dignified burial for Séléné, by contacting her 2 magician friends.

**Clues**

- A **strange crater** is present where a rock fell not far from the cottage, during the explosion.
- Various tools and ingredients (including poisons) can be found in the alchemist's cottage.
- Her enormous **Grimoire of Life** is missing, where she kept all her writings and precious documents, as well as her diary.
- A few pieces of rock, metal and crystal have also disappeared from the shelves.
- The Ancestral Tortoise can recount the events surrounding the mining explosion, from the alchemist's point of view.


1 - The witcher is on his way to the village
-----------------------------------------------

Tracking shows that the rider left directly in the direction of the village, which is **12 kilometres** away.

An exploration of the rock massifs reveals the small mining diggings ravaged by the explosion, with the 3 graves of the explorers.
If the graves are dug up, the explorers' papers turn out to be missing.

The witcher takes a nap at the hunting camp.
He sleeps, protected by a small **alarm automaton** which detects animal presences in the vicinity.
All his gear is on his horse, including his water flask.
Easily awakened by the presence of the sapients, the witcher gets back on the road.

If there is a poisoning attempt, he hurriedly pulls a pebble (a "bezoar") out of his belt pouch to cure himself.

When he arrives at the village, inhabitants come running to greet him.



2 - The witcher is cheered by the villagers on his return
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Abélard announces to everyone that he has accomplished his mission.

Then, away from the crowd, he shows Séléné's head to the notables of the village.

**Colbert the apothecary**, alone, has misty eyes. He asks to go and bury the head according to custom, so that peace may return, which he is granted (now or later).

Swept along by the crowd, Abélard leaves his horse to be taken to the stable, warning that nobody must touch its load, which may be **magically unstable**.
Joséphine insists on carrying out this task, and sends **Rose** - daughter of **Hannibal Monir the innkeeper** - back to look after the guests.

Abélard goes for a drink at the inn with the villagers, humbly recounts his adventure, admits that he did not destroy the tower straight away for fear of the reaction of the nocturnal animals, and courteously flirts with the innkeeper's daughter.
The innkeeper takes a very dim view of this, and pulls his daughter aside to scold her, saying that she had better not fall for an adventurer who will toy with her and get her pregnant before abandoning her.

Meanwhile, Joséphine discreetly goes to retrieve the **Grimoire of Life** from the horse's saddlebags, and either spirits it away entirely, or takes only its last pages and the notebooks inserted in it. She stops by the Town Hall to deposit her loot in her personal safe.

Abélard pulls out of his own satchel various **precious crystals and metals** brought back from the alchemist's tower, recalls that he had only been promised half a pound of gold for his good services, and helps himself by cutting off a piece of one of the ingots thus plundered. He returns the rest of the loot to the village, "to which it rightfully belongs", thereby demonstrating his probity.

Abélard then goes to unload the large saddlebags from his horse, then finishes his night in his room at the inn.

The villagers set about preparing a small banquet in honour of the witcher.

Meanwhile the apothecary finishes burying the alchemist's head, and is forthcoming with the animals if they contact him. He says he is desperate and incredulous about the situation, but discreet, because he does not want the village to turn against him too. He tells the animals to come and talk away from prying eyes in his shop, later in the afternoon, while he himself mingles with the festivities so as not to attract attention.



3 - The witcher makes his speech and sows discord
-----------------------------------------------------------

At noon the banquet of honour takes place, in the inner courtyard of the inn.

The witcher reveals his plans for what comes next: after a decade of roaming the roads, he has fallen in love with this land (as well as with one of its inhabitants, one gathers), and fully intends to settle there for good. He even stands for election to the post of **burgomaster**, arguing that he will be able to bring both renown and comfort to the village (drawing water from the hot spring, putting up solar mills...), while preserving the tranquillity that makes its charm.

The burgomaster, outraged by events, briefly summons the notary and his daughter upstairs, to humiliate the latter and demand a solution.
Joséphine, very cold, tells him that she has a plan, but that they must keep up appearances for now, and both wait for her discreetly after nightfall, with handguns, in the **old stable** of the village. In the meantime, she reminds them that as many traces of the alchemist as possible (including her dwelling) must be made to disappear, to cover the trail.

Abélard, who has gone out for some air, is timidly joined by Rose, the innkeeper's daughter. He then puts a temporary end to his courtship, arguing that he does not want his advances to be misinterpreted by the village, that this is already earning him the enmity of the innkeeper and of a few young men, and that he prefers to "let the dust settle" before the two of them see each other again. Understanding, but with tears in her eyes, Rose goes back to serving.

The burgomaster brings up again the subject of the alchemist's **undestroyed tower**. Abélard agrees to set off again at once, with whichever villagers are willing, to destroy what remains of the alchemist's den. Galvanised, a dozen villagers take up pitchforks and scythes and follow him, including the notary (on the burgomaster's orders).



3 - The villagers interrupt the alchemist's funeral
--------------------------------------------------------------

The Ancestral Tortoise warns the sapient animals that the Enchantress and the Druid have arrived, and that they are holding a cremation ceremony with many animals that have come running.
They wonder whether to burn the tower, since in any case Séléné had already passed on to each of them, as she went along, the well-established knowledge to be handed down to future apprentices.

If the 2 mourners are still there when the villagers arrive, they are taken prisoner. Otherwise, Abélard may set off in pursuit of them and take them prisoner, if they have not been sufficiently warned.

If the tower has not yet burned, Abélard first insists on searching it, and helps to find a few rare handwritten parchments, to bring them back to the village; then the dwelling is set on fire. If they are not prisoners, the 2 magicians join the Ancestral Tortoise in a hiding place in the forest.

Meanwhile, in the village, in his shop, **the apothecary** can explain to the animals what he knows of the story: the rocks that fell on the village, and the threatening parchment delivered to the burgomaster - by a green-billed toucan, it seems (only the burgomaster saw it). He can ask the burgomaster for this parchment, "for the village archives", if prompted, because he is also the **archivist**.

Joséphine arrives at the apothecary's, and requests a powerful narcotic potion, supposedly "because her father has insomnia and asked to try it". The apothecary hesitates, but ends up giving it to her, if the animals do not convince him otherwise.

In return he asks for the key to the telegraph office (for which he is ALSO responsible), a key he had agreed to entrust to the burgomaster. Joséphine dodges the question, saying that they will bring it back soon, but that this alchemist business may still require urgent communications outside the telegraph office's meagre opening hours (the rest of the time, there is an **"answering machine"**); she states that she was just on her way there, at Abélard's request, to inform the **Order of Witchers** of the latest events and avoid misunderstandings.

At the telegraph office, Joséphine does indeed send this **status message**; but she also sends **another message**, to the Minéralys Company, summarising the situation in very neutral terms, and saying that she is acting so that "the rockfall affair" remains unsolved.

Joséphine then goes to work in her office at the Town Hall, and checks in her personal safe for the presence of a small, very heavy and extremely well-locked metal casket (impossible to open). The animals can potentially see her secret combination.


4 - The evening brings dramatic twists
---------------------------------------------------

The villagers are all back in Sylvorann, and a last light meal takes place in the evening.

If the **Grimoire of Life** has been spirited away/altered by Joséphine, and Abélard is informed of it or realises it, he feigns anger and worry, and calls for it to be found "in order to avoid catastrophes".

If, at some point, Abélard is confronted with the fact that the handwriting of Séléné's **forged** threatening **letter** does not match that found on the other manuscripts, then he launches into a counter-investigation, appearing increasingly troubled.

Depending on the various pieces of information the apothecary may bring back, the first level of **conspiracy** is, or is not, exposed; if it is, both Joséphine and Abélard *officially* distance themselves from the burgomaster, without accusing him either. Joséphine can point out that the green-billed **toucan** supposedly belongs to the Enchantress Dorina.

Joséphine reassures her father - who is nonetheless far from at ease - as best she can, and confirms to him that their evening **rendezvous** still stands.

At some point, Abélard announces that he needs to think alone, and that he is going to bathe at the **hot spring** near the forest - let no one follow him!

This gives the innkeeper's daughter, Rose, the idea of joining him there; but Joséphine sees the manoeuvre coming, and warns the innkeeper so that he keeps an eye on his daughter, and on the other damsels of the village.

The burgomaster and the notary go as agreed to the **old stable** to wait for Joséphine.

But Joséphine makes her way to the bathing witcher by roundabout paths. They have a "credible" conversation, in which Joséphine says she wants to raise the subject of the forthcoming elections to the post of Burgomaster, while Abélard claims not to want this meeting, because it may set tongues wagging.

Once the witcher has checked that no human is nearby, thanks to the alarm automaton, they drop their **pretence** and speak frankly to each other.

- Joséphine criticises the complications introduced by flirting with the innkeeper's daughter; Abélard says he is sincere in his courtship but has put all that on hold.
- They brief each other on the cleaning up of the evidence, in the forest and in the Grimoire of Life, of "the rockfall affair".
- Then Joséphine pulls a metal jaw out of her bag (taken from the heavy metal casket), and they stage, by scarifying the witcher's chest until it bleeds, the fact that he was supposedly attacked with this strange weapon.

Joséphine then goes to hide the **bloodied jaw** in the burgomaster's office; shortly afterwards, the witcher runs half-naked to the village, roaring that he has been attacked by two men who tried to slit his throat with a strange weapon. He gets himself taken to the inn, while villagers organise a sort of manhunt/search.

The burgomaster and the notary hear the hubbub, but hesitate - or not - to come out of their hiding place.

Depending on the actions taken by the sapients during this last night of turmoil, many conclusions are possible, with the death or conviction of certain notables, and the re-establishment - or not - of a blood pact between the village and the forest.
