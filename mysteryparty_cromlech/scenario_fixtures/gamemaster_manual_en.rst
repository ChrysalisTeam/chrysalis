Synopsis
===============

The Cromlech is an unclassifiable building, half library, half museum, which has become the haunt of the neighbourhood's students.

But one fine morning the police turn up in force: one of the regulars has disappeared, along with the gladius from the ancient treasure.

An investigation is needed, and fast, for the case could soon spin out of control...


Full Story (SPOILERS)
===================================

*This game borrows the atmosphere (but not the lore) of Lovecraft and the Cthulhu Mythos. The players' action takes place in a seemingly ordinary library, which however houses cursed artefacts from the pre-Gallo-Roman era.*

In antiquity, following the defeat at Alesia (52 BC), the Gauls were conquered by Julius Caesar. But not all of them. Rebel tribes kept up the fight, and took refuge in the oppidum of Uxellodunum (in the uplands of Quercy). Starved out by the Roman siege, they ended up surrendering in their turn.

But a few druids, who had come from Armorica, set up one last act of vengeance. Taking refuge in a cave with some fugitives, they invoked their tutelary god, {% symbol "Toutatis" for "bloodthirsty_god_name" %}, through dark sorcery; with the aim of bringing forth a mythical warrior, who would reverse the course of events and bring them victory.

However, the gestation of this entity proved longer and more complex than expected. The ablutions and rituals - necessary for the operation to run smoothly - were interrupted many times throughout History.

So much so that at the very beginning of the 20th century, this legendary creature has still not become embodied. The artefacts bearing the enchantment (grimoire, gladius, cromlech model...) are now "hidden in plain sight", in an unusual library-museum that mainly serves as a workspace for the local students. To avoid legal trouble, the manager of the place, the "Curator", passes these museum pieces off as mere replicas.

But a young "Hero", the child of an Italian diplomat and passionate about ancient esotericism, is on the trail of this invocation. He discreetly spies on the museum, trying to find out who knows about this phenomenon, and who is friend or foe. Along the way, he befriends a student reporter, whom he partly lets in on the secret.

On the day of the crime, at dawn, the Hero catches the museum's Curator performing magic rituals on the artefacts. The confrontation turns into a brawl. The Hero defends himself with the Gaulish gladius, wounding his opponent on the left hand. Exasperated, the Curator shoots him right in the belly; then leaves him lying near the artefacts, revolver in hand - to make it look like a suicide - before fleeing to show his face in public at a society event. {% xfact "le conservateur a été blessé à la main gauche par le héros" %} {% xfact "le conservateur a tiré au pistolet sur le héros" %}

When the library-museum opens at the usual time, nothing unusual is noticed. The guide-librarian who runs the place is merely upset by a huge "ink stain" near the artefacts.

But the Reporter, who had an appointment with the Hero, grows worried. He notices that the gladius is no longer in its scabbard, and that the stain is made of dried blood. So he raises the alarm, informing the Italian diplomat along the way of the probable disappearance of her child.

As a result, a whole crowd descends on the premises: the city's Police Inspector, and several teams of private detectives "specialised in the paranormal" (the players).

The detective-players therefore investigate on the premises, questioning the various witnesses in this case in groups, in parallel.
They receive very different, contradictory points of view on the library-museum, the Hero, and the various witnesses. They must sort truth from falsehood.

The further they progress through this interrogation rotation, the more clues have accumulated in the meantime, and so the better placed the players are to "corner" a witness. Each group will thus have different access to the crucial information and the explosive revelations.
The Police Inspector steps in from time to time, to point out a troubling detail or a contradiction.

All the while, disturbing phenomena begin to manifest (sounds, lights...). For the Hero has in fact healed himself thanks to a healing potion, and has gone off to face the nascent Monster before it is too late, in a parallel reality accessible via the miniature cromlech. {% xitem "potion de guérison utilisée" is needed %}

A Druid's spectre appears to some of the players (the psykers) to turn them against the Hero, lying about the ongoing gestation - which has in fact become irreversibly uncontrollable and demonic.

There are two different endings, depending on whether the majority of players side with the Reporter (in favour of the Hero), or with the Curator (in favour of the Druid and the Monster):

- Either the Hero slays the gestating beast and returns to the world of the living; the world is then saved.
- Or the Hero fails, flees, and natural disasters (of demonic origin) then rain down on Rome and the rest of Italy.

**Important:**

There is deliberately some ambiguity as to which is the "right" final choice for the players.
But they have a few clues to help them decide:

- Their character sheet states that the abnormal volcanic phenomena in Italy began long ago, at about the time the Curator recovered the artefacts;
- The Hero's attitude suggests that he genuinely felt in danger from the very start of his investigation;
- The study of the artefacts points to a link with {% symbol "Toutatis" for "bloodthirsty_god_name" %}, the bloodthirsty warrior god of the Gauls, and a rejection of the more peaceful gods and goddesses of the Celtic pantheon.


NPC Actor Descriptions
===========================================

The Curator of the Cromlech
    Sober but elegant, well-off, withdrawn, obtuse, anxious, aggressive when put on the spot. Central character of the plot.

The Guide and librarian of the Cromlech
    Jovial, helpful, a jack-of-all-trades, full of imagination, fairly cultured and eloquent, a little absent-minded and prone to lateness, but relatively rigorous in shelving the books.

The Reporter, a journalism student
    Enterprising, determined, perceptive, rational, devoted to the investigators.

The Druid who haunts the Cromlech
    Silent, mysterious, haughty; suddenly reveals himself to a player (psyker) by showing wide, mad eyes and panting out extravagant, distressing or unintelligible sentences.

The Police Inspector
    Supervises the operations. In the manner of "Sherlock Holmes", steps in from time to time to share a few brilliant observations and intuitions with the players.


Description of the Museum Treasure
===========================================

When it is their turn, instead of questioning one of the witnesses, the players go and inspect the museum's ancient artefacts.

An **archaeology expert** may be present on the premises to tell them in detail what he knows about each of the objects present; or else they have direct access to the document **The Cromlech Treasure**.

In reality:

- The cromlech model is the nerve centre of the curse; it contains an altar emitting lightning flashes, and a magic portal leading to the parallel world in which the divine entity is developing. The symbols of 3 benevolent Gaulish deities are carved on the stone circle. {% xitem "maquette de Cromlech et de portail magique" is needed %}
- The Roman armour, helmet and shield are offerings in support of the druids' invocation. The gladius, for its part, was taken by the Hero into the parallel world. The helmet may contain a skull (plain, then bewitched?), if the audience is mature enough. {% xitem "armure, casque et bouclier romains" is needed %} {% xitem "crâne électronique et/ou normal, en option, pour mettre dans le casque romain" is needed %}
- The grimoire is actually fake; it is there mainly for atmosphere, but can reveal an extra clue inside it in mid-game. {% xitem "grimoire scellé à couverture de bois" is needed %}
- A wooden chest, with a 4-digit padlock, is present. It contains the following items. {% xitem "Coffre en bois avec cadenas à 4 chiffres" is needed %}

  - A black curse tablet (defixio), i.e. a spell tablet, bearing the sigil of the Gaulish god of war. {% xitem "tablette noire de défixion" is needed %}
  - A cryptex (containing the medieval parchment). {% xitem "cryptex contenant le parchemin médiéval" is needed %}
