Synopsis
===============

The Cromlech is an unclassifiable building, half library, half museum, which has become the haunt of the neighbourhood's students.

But one fine morning the police turn up in force: one of the regulars has disappeared, along with the gladius from the ancient treasure.

An investigation is needed, and fast, for the case could soon spin out of control...


Full Story (SPOILERS)
===================================

*This game borrows the atmosphere (but not the lore) of Lovecraft and the Cthulhu Mythos. The players' action takes place in a seemingly ordinary library, which however houses cursed artefacts from the pre-Gallo-Roman era.*

In antiquity, following the defeat at Alesia (52 BC), the Gauls were conquered by Julius Caesar. But not all of them. Rebel tribes kept up the fight, and took refuge in the oppidum of Uxellodunum (in the uplands of Quercy). Cut off from their water by the Roman siege, they ended up surrendering in their turn.

But a few druids, who had come from Armorica, set up one last act of vengeance. Taking refuge in a cave with some fugitives, they invoked their tutelary god, {% symbol "Toutatis" for "bloodthirsty_god_name" %}, through dark sorcery; with the aim of bringing forth a mythical warrior, who would reverse the course of events and bring them victory.

However, the gestation of this entity proved longer and more complex than expected. The ablutions and rituals - necessary for the operation to run smoothly - were interrupted many times throughout History.

So much so that at the very beginning of the 20th century, this legendary creature has still not become embodied. The artefacts bearing the enchantment (grimoire, gladius, cromlech model...) are now "hidden in plain sight", in an unusual library-museum that mainly serves as a workspace for the local students. To avoid legal trouble, the manager of the place, the "Curator", passes these museum pieces off as mere replicas.

But a young "Hero", the child of an Italian diplomat and passionate about ancient esotericism, is on the trail of this invocation. He discreetly spies on the museum, trying to find out who knows about this phenomenon, and who is friend or foe. Along the way, he befriends a student reporter, whom he partly lets in on the secret.

On the day of the crime, at dawn, the Hero catches the museum's Curator performing magic rituals on the artefacts. The confrontation turns into a brawl. The Hero defends himself with the ancient gladius, wounding his opponent on the left hand. Exasperated, the Curator shoots him right in the belly; then leaves him lying near the artefacts, revolver in hand - to make it look like a suicide - before fleeing to show his face in public at a society event. {% xfact "le conservateur a été blessé à la main gauche par le héros" %} {% xfact "le conservateur a tiré au pistolet sur le héros" %}

When the library-museum opens at the usual time, nothing unusual is noticed. The guide-librarian who runs the place is merely upset by a huge "ink stain" near the artefacts.

But the Reporter, who had an appointment with the Hero, grows worried. He notices that the gladius is no longer in its scabbard, and that the stain is made of dried blood. So he raises the alarm, informing the Italian diplomat along the way of the probable disappearance of her child.

As a result, a whole crowd descends on the premises: the city's Police Inspector, and several teams of private detectives "specialised in the paranormal" (the players).

The detective-players therefore investigate on the premises, questioning the various witnesses in this case in groups, in parallel.
They receive very different, contradictory points of view on the library-museum, the Hero, and the various witnesses. They must sort truth from falsehood.

The further they progress through this interrogation rotation, the more clues have accumulated in the meantime, and so the better placed the players are to "corner" a witness. Each group will thus have different access to the crucial information and the explosive revelations.
The Police Inspector steps in from time to time, to point out a troubling detail or a contradiction.

All the while, disturbing phenomena begin to manifest (sounds, lights...). For the Hero has in fact healed himself thanks to a {% symbol "healing potion" for "healing_potion" %}, and has gone off to face the nascent Monster before it is too late, in a parallel reality accessible via the miniature cromlech. {% xitem "potion de guérison utilisée" is needed %}

A Druid's spectre appears to some of the players (the psykers) to turn them against the Hero, lying about the ongoing gestation - which has in fact become irreversibly uncontrollable and demonic.

There are two different endings, depending on whether the majority of players side with the Reporter (in favour of the Hero), or with the Curator (in favour of the Druid and the Monster); the "Game Flow" sheet gives the rule for counting the two camps during the final scene, and Andrea's two closing speeches:

- Either the Hero slays the gestating beast and returns to the world of the living; the world is then saved.
- Or the Hero fails, flees, and natural disasters (of demonic origin) then rain down on Rome and the rest of Italy.

**Important:**

There is deliberately some ambiguity as to which is the "right" final choice for the players.
But they have a few clues to help them decide:

- Their character sheet states that the abnormal volcanic phenomena in Italy began about {% symbol "1 year" for "italian_earthquakes_start_delay" %} ago, shortly after the Curator recovered the artefacts: the newspaper article states that he took them out of the family cellar on his father's death, {% symbol "15 months" for "curator_father_death_delay" %} ago;
- The Hero's attitude suggests that he genuinely felt in danger from the very start of his investigation;
- The study of the artefacts points to a link with {% symbol "Toutatis" for "bloodthirsty_god_name" %}, the bloodthirsty warrior god of the Gauls, and a rejection of the more peaceful gods and goddesses of the Celtic pantheon.


NPC Actor Descriptions
===========================================

The Curator of the Cromlech
    Sober but elegant, well-off, withdrawn, obtuse, anxious, aggressive when put on the spot. Central character of the plot.

The Guide and librarian of the Cromlech
    Jovial, helpful, a jack-of-all-trades, full of imagination, fairly cultured and eloquent, a little absent-minded and prone to lateness, but relatively rigorous in shelving the books.

The Reporter, a journalism student
    Enterprising, determined, perceptive, rational, devoted to the investigators.

The Druid who haunts the Cromlech
    Silent, mysterious, haughty; suddenly reveals himself to a player (psyker) by showing wide, mad eyes and panting out extravagant, distressing or unintelligible sentences.

The Police Inspector
    Supervises the operations. In the manner of "Sherlock Holmes", steps in from time to time to share a few brilliant observations and intuitions with the players. This role is played by the GM in person (so it has no character sheet): all the Inspector's lines and interventions are in the "Game Flow" sheet, and the Inspector is the one who hands the documents over to the players.

Professor Gustave Bloch, archaeology expert (optional)
    Eccentric, agitated, erudite, sceptical about legends. Posted at the Treasure during the four interviews, he tells the players in detail what is known about the artefacts (relying for this on the document "The Cromlech Treasure"); during the interlude, he gives his History lesson (see the "Game Flow" sheet). If no NPC actor is available for this role, the Treasure works with the document alone, and the GM plays the professor in person for the duration of the lesson (the Inspector having gone off "to fetch him").

{% symbol "Andrea di Revel" for "hero_name" %}, the Hero (final appearance)
    A 20-year-old young Italian, passionate, feverish, brave. He only appears at the very end of the final scene, to announce the ending (see the "Game Flow" sheet). He is played by an extra NPC actor, who stays hidden until then, or failing that by Professor Bloch's actor, who changes costume during the final huddles. His appearance is to be described to the other NPC actors during their briefing, so that they all describe him in the same way to the players (to be adapted to his actor): slim, brown hair, elegant student clothes (waistcoat, white shirt), a bracelet adorned with old Celtic symbols on his wrist, a slight Italian accent, left-handed. For his final appearance: a torn shirt stained with blood at the belly, a blackened face, and the Roman gladius in hand if he is victorious.

*Note: the NPC actors' characters are men in all the documents (police identity sheets, newspaper article, the other NPC actors' sheets). An actress can perfectly well play one of them as a man; if you prefer to make the character a woman (hence the "Mr/Mrs" of some sheets), tell the other NPC actors, and correct by hand the documents handed out to the players.*


Description of the Museum Treasure
===========================================

When it is their turn, instead of questioning one of the witnesses, the players go and inspect the museum's ancient artefacts.

Professor Gustave Bloch, an **archaeology expert**, may be present on the premises to tell them in detail what he knows about each of the objects present; or else they have direct access to the first page of the document **The Cromlech Treasure**, laid near the artefacts; the GM then keeps the second page ("Hidden Artefacts"), and reads each of its paragraphs to the players when they find the corresponding object.

In reality:

- The cromlech model is the nerve centre of the curse; it contains an altar emitting lightning flashes, and a magic portal leading to the parallel world in which the divine entity is developing. The symbols of the 6 benevolent Gaulish deities of the mythology paper (all but {% symbol "Toutatis" for "bloodthirsty_god_name" %}) are carved, as six runes, on the stone circle: they do not invoke these deities, they repel them (see the medieval letter in the cryptex), whereas the curse tablet bears the sigil of {% symbol "Toutatis" for "bloodthirsty_god_name" %}. {% xitem "maquette de Cromlech et de portail magique" is needed %}
- The Roman armour, helmet and shield are offerings in support of the druids' invocation. The gladius, for its part, was taken by the Hero into the parallel world. The helmet may contain a skull (plain, then bewitched?), if the audience is mature enough. {% xitem "armure, casque et bouclier romains" is needed %} {% xitem "crâne électronique et/ou normal, en option, pour mettre dans le casque romain" is needed %}
- The grimoire is fake: it is not a real book, but a carved block of wood which does not open, and has no cavity. It bears the enchantment like the other artefacts, but it is there mainly for atmosphere; in mid-game, it starts vibrating, then an extra clue magically appears under its wooden cover (see the Game Flow). {% xitem "grimoire scellé à couverture de bois" is needed %}
- The {% xitem "papier avec les symboles de la mythologie gauloise" is needed %}paper with the symbols of Gaulish mythology (game_clues document) is laid near the model from the start of the game, and Professor Gustave Bloch gives each group a copy of it during the interlude: it identifies the six runes of the stone circle and the sigil of the curse tablet, which is essential to make use of the medieval letter in the cryptex, and hence for the final choice.
- A wooden chest, with a 4-digit padlock, is present. It contains the following items. {% xitem "Coffre en bois avec cadenas à 4 chiffres" is needed %}

  - A black curse tablet (defixio), i.e. a spell tablet, bearing the sigil of the Gaulish god of war. {% xitem "tablette noire de défixion" is needed %}
  - A cryptex (containing the medieval parchment). {% xitem "cryptex contenant le parchemin médiéval" is needed %}

**Clue labels**: some objects bear a small label, which describes what the detectives' paranormal senses perceive of that object (the witnesses cannot see these labels). Texts to copy onto these labels:

- Used {% symbol "healing potion" for "healing_potion" %} (on the floor, near the bloodstain): *"Empty phial, still warm. An aura of healing emanates from it: someone drank this potion barely a few hours ago."*
- Cromlech model: *"An intense energy pulses at the centre of the model, like a beating heart, behind the little wooden door."*
- Grimoire: *"This block of wood is charged with very ancient magic. It seems to be listening."*
- Roman armour, helmet and shield: *"Echoes of battle reach you: cries in Latin and in Gaulish, the clash of weapons... and blood shed as an offering."*
- Black curse tablet (in the chest): *"Icy to the touch. A bloodthirsty murmur seems to emanate from it."*


Preparation and logistics
===========================================

Players and groups
    The players form **4 groups** of detectives, one per investigation station, of 3 to 6 players each (ideally 4 or 5). In each group, three players receive a special role: clerk, psyker and intimidator (see the "Paranormal Detectives" sheet); the groups can be named A, B, C and D.

Locations
    - The Treasure Room, where the artefacts are displayed; the Treasure is inspected there during the interviews, and the final scene is played there.
    - Three interview spaces, one per witness (Guide, Reporter, Curator), far enough apart for the groups not to overhear one another; the Guide's and the Curator's must be in two different rooms.
    - A large room, outside the Treasure Room, for the welcome, the investigation updates, the interlude and the final huddles.
    - A hiding place near the Treasure Room, where Andrea's actor waits during the final scene.

NPC actors' arrival
    The NPC actors arrive about **45 minutes before the players**, for their briefing (see the "Game Flow" sheet), costumes and make-up, and the setting up of the Treasure.

Timetable (about 2 hours)
    - Out-of-game briefing of the players: 10 min.
    - Welcome by the Inspector, newspaper article and initial investigation report: 5 min.
    - 4 simultaneous interviews of 12 min each.
    - After each of the first 3 interviews, an investigation update: 5 min, the time to gather the groups in the large room, give them the update, then send them to their next station.
    - Interlude (Professor Bloch's History lesson), between the 2nd update and the 3rd interview: 10 min.
    - Final huddles: 10 min.
    - Final scene: about 5 min (the length of the soundtrack).
    - Debriefing: 10 min.

During an interview
    The GM rings the **gong** after 12 minutes. In the 2nd and 3rd interviews, the Inspector steps in at about the 4th minute with the Curator, then at about the 8th minute with the Guide. In the 4th interview, the witnesses start to ramble from the 6th minute. The Druid steps in during the 1st, 2nd and 4th interviews (see his sheet).

Group rotation
    Each group goes once through each station, in the cyclic order Guide, Reporter, Curator, Treasure:

    ===========  =====  ========  =======  ========
    Interview    Guide  Reporter  Curator  Treasure
    ===========  =====  ========  =======  ========
    1st          A      B         C        D
    2nd          D      A         B        C
    3rd          C      D         A        B
    4th          B      C         D        A
    ===========  =====  ========  =======  ========

    So the Druid meets each group's psyker once: those of group D (at the Guide) and group B (at the Curator) in the 2nd interview, those of group C (at the Reporter) and group A (at the Treasure) in the 4th.

Documents to hand out
    - Out-of-game briefing: one "Paranormal Detectives" sheet per player; an armband for each psyker, paper and a pen for each clerk.
    - Welcome: to each group, the newspaper article about the Cromlech, and the first part of the police reports document (identity sheets of the people involved and initial investigation report).
    - After the 1st, 2nd and 3rd interviews: to each group, investigation update #1, #2 then #3 (the police reports document is to be printed in 4 copies, then cut up between the reports).
    - At the Treasure: the first page of the document "The Cromlech Treasure" (if Professor Bloch is not there) and the paper with the symbols of Gaulish mythology, laid near the artefacts; the medieval letter (game_clues document), rolled up in the cryptex; and, from the 4th interview, Andrea's message slipped under the grimoire's cover.
    - Interlude: to each group, the document "The Siege of Uxellodunum" and a copy of the paper with the symbols of Gaulish mythology.
    - NPC actors: each their own sheet; the Guide also receives a copy of "The Siege of Uxellodunum", which his sheet refers to, and the Treasure's expert the full "The Cromlech Treasure" document.

Puzzle solutions
    - **Wooden chest padlock**: code {% symbol "2681" for "wooden_chest_code" %}. Update #1 reveals the piece of paper hidden in the Curator's wallet (*"clue: birth year reversed"*), and his identity sheet gives his year of birth, 1862: reversed, it gives 2681.
    - **Cryptex**: password "{% symbol "secret" for "cryptex_code" %}". Update #2 reveals the inscription on the censer, *"the cryptex code is secret"*, which must be taken literally. If the players are stuck, the Inspector can hint: "What if this inscription meant exactly what it says?"
    - The Curator knows both codes; he does not give them of his own accord, but an intimidator can make him confirm a guess.
    - The coded message on the envelope is deciphered by the police themselves (update #3).
    - The final choice is made by counting the two camps during the final scene (see the "Game Flow" sheet).
